The essentials

Four categories, different contexts

These short overviews are not a complete account of any medicine or substance. They are a place to begin—not a basis for self-treatment.

01Broad category

Peptide compounds

Peptides are short chains of amino acids. The term covers many different molecules; some have specific approved medical uses, while others are investigational or sold without approval.

Claims made about one peptide should not be assumed to apply to another. Products marketed online may not have verified identity, strength, purity, or safety.

02Hormones & medicines

Androgen hormones & related compounds

Testosterone is an androgen hormone and an anabolic-androgenic steroid. Clinicians may prescribe it for specific diagnosed conditions; other anabolic steroids are related substances that can affect many body systems.

Nonmedical use can disrupt natural hormone production and may affect fertility, mood, blood pressure, cholesterol, heart, or liver health. Risks vary by substance and person.

03Unapproved for bodybuilding

Investigational androgen modulators

Selective androgen receptor modulators are a group of investigational compounds. They are not approved medicines for bodybuilding.

Long-term effects in people are not well understood. The U.S. Anti-Doping Agency notes that these compounds have not been approved for human use and that serious health concerns have been reported.

Read USADA's overview ↗
04Prescription hormone

Growth hormone therapies

Human growth hormone is prescribed for certain diagnosed conditions. It is not the same as an anabolic steroid, and a medical prescription has a specific clinical context.

Use without medical supervision can cause harm, including fluid retention, joint or muscle pain, and changes in blood glucose. A clinician can assess personal risks and alternatives.

Keep your health in view

Before making a health decision

General information cannot replace advice based on your health history, medicines, and circumstances.

A

Talk with a licensed professional

Ask a licensed clinician or pharmacist about evidence, interactions, monitoring, and legal status where you live.

B

Be cautious with online claims

Marketing language and a product label do not establish that contents are authentic, safe, or approved.

C

Share the full picture

Tell your healthcare professional about medicines, supplements, and substances you take or are considering.
